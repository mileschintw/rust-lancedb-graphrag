## Summary

The S2 reader comparison (#54) could print two misleading figures. Both are fixed in `eval/src/lancet_eval/lever_comparison.py`.

1. **deepseek priced at its list price.** `_reader_price` priced the carried engine arm at `measure`'s budget ceiling: 0.14 in, about 10x the 0.0137 listing. `measure.py` gets one named list-price pair, `GENERATION_LIST_INPUT_PRICE_PER_1M = 0.0137` and `GENERATION_LIST_OUTPUT_PRICE_PER_1M = 1.28`, the 2026-10-09 listing it already records. The carried arm is now costed at that pair, matching D-257's "sync list price × tokens for every reader". The ceiling constants are unchanged and still bound what a drive may spend. The row's price source now reads `measure.GENERATION_LIST_*_PRICE_PER_1M`.
2. **One question set for every reader row.** A reader record carries none of the engine clauses, so its replay of a rerank-degraded context was `ok`. The deepseek row dropped the question while the Claude rows kept it. The new `_reader_population` builds each group's common population: the G questions every reader of the group is ok on. Every reader's `answer_usable` and cost per answered question run over that set.
   - The group payload gains `population: {n, n_g, drops}`. Each entry in `drops` gives one arm's own drops, counted the way `_exclusions` counts them: `own_failure`, `own_provenance`, `of_which_rerank_degrade`.
   - The rendered reader table gains an `excluded` column.
   - A sentence under the table names each arm's drops and why, for example "`hybrid+rerank+metadata` drops 2 (provenance 2, of which rerank degraded 2)". It also says the paired differences keep their own pairwise populations.
   - `COST_BASIS` says the same.

The paired rows (`_reader_pair`) and the null guards are unchanged. The batch spend still covers every replay record, apart from the comparison.
