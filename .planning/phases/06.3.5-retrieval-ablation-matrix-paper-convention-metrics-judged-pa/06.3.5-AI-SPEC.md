## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid. Inherited from `06.3.4.1-AI-SPEC.md` §1: a multi-hop RAG / GraphRAG system under test
(Rust engine, LanceDB hybrid retrieval with RRF, entity/edge graph, OpenRouter generation) plus its Python
`lancet-eval` harness.

**Description (phase delta only):**
06.3.5 measures the shipped system rather than changing its answers. It adds a `retrieval_mode` request field
(`dense_only` / `bm25_only` / `hybrid`, D-99) and an opt-in pre-truncation ranking field (D-100), then drives
four arms (`dense-only`, `bm25-only`, `hybrid`, `hybrid+graph`, D-101) interleaved over a committed held-out
split: 308 G questions plus 43 nulls (D-105, D-107). It reports MultiHop-RAG paper-convention retrieval
metrics (Hits@4, Hits@10, MRR@10, MAP@10, D-102) next to the harness's own metrics, judges every judgeable
record of every arm (D-112), and calibrates the judge against a 20-item blinded human slice with a
pre-committed trust floor (D-113, D-114). "Good" means a run of record whose per-arm deltas against
`hybrid` are pre-registered (D-111), paired, Holm-corrected, and traceable to records whose arm provenance
is verified on the wire, and whose judged numbers carry a calibration label a reader can act on.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. **The judged deltas measure abstention, not grounding.** About 40% of drive-2 answers end in
   `Answer: Insufficient information` with citations attached, so `_is_judgeable` admits them. An answer
   with no claims sits at the rubric ceiling. If that is not handled, an arm that abstains more looks
   better on groundedness and faithfulness, and a ceiling-heavy calibration slice makes QWK unstable.
2. **The calibration is not blind, or comes too late.** Judged aggregates or per-arm judged deltas are
   visible before the owner's scores are committed (D-113). The worksheet leaks the arm or the verdict.
   Or the floor is chosen after agreement is seen (D-114, D-73).
3. **The numbers are "paper-convention" in name only.** The matching rule, the MAP@10 formula, the
   denominator or the null-query handling diverges from the official `retrieval_evaluate.py` without being
   disclosed, so the paper reference row (D-103) is compared against a different metric.
4. **Arm provenance fails.** A `dense_only` or `bm25_only` request silently runs hybrid. The D-100 ranking
   field disagrees with the final ranking it claims to precede. Or the `hybrid` default stops being
   byte-identical to the graph-off golden (D-99). Any of these turns an arm delta into an artefact.
5. **Forking paths.** Primaries, the Holm family, the split, caps or the QWK floor are chosen or changed
   after held-out data is seen, or new arms are run on the dev set (D-106, D-111, D-105).

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **A 20-item QWK crosses or misses the floor by chance.** At n = 20 the point estimate's 5th–95th percentile range
   was 0.18–0.41 wide across the synthetic spread-marginal scenarios, and wider the lower the agreement
   **[computed]**. Examples: a long-run QWK of 0.764
   gave 0.569–0.884; a long-run QWK of 0.649 gave 0.403–0.810. So an "uncalibrated" or a "trusted" label on one slice
   is weak evidence either way. The published sample-size heuristic is at least 30 comparisons (McHugh 2012). The
   practitioner response is to disclose n and the CI, and to treat "below the floor" as "not shown", not as "shown
   bad". Redrawing or enlarging the slice after the result is seen is not a valid response (forking path, D-114).
2. **Kappa collapses on a ceiling-dominated slice, and is inflated by a moderate ceiling.** This is the paradox Feinstein &
   Cicchetti (1990) describe: "a high value of p0 can be drastically lowered by a substantial imbalance in the
   table's marginal totals". **[computed]** 18/20 exact agreement at 5, with one 4/5 swap, gives QWK = −0.053, and its
   bootstrap CI (−0.136, 0.000) silently drops 12.6% of resamples as undefined. When 80% of items sit at the joint
   ceiling, 3.6% of 20-item slices have **no defined QWK**, and the 5th-percentile estimate is 0.46. The opposite
   effect is equally domain-specific. A 40% joint-ceiling block added to otherwise spread ratings *raised*
   population QWK (0.841 → 0.895), because exact agreements at 5 count as agreement on an extreme category. So
   ceiling-heavy abstention items in the slice can either make the floor impossible to pass or flatter it. The
   direction depends on how the rest of the slice is spread. The abstention policy has to be fixed in the
   judgeable predicate **before** the draw.
3. **Grounding scores measure abstention.** An `Answer: Insufficient information` reply is not claim-free. It carries
   meta-claims about the evidence, for example "Evidence [2] … does not directly address …". The v1 rubric says
   nothing about abstentions (verified facts). The judge may therefore score these anywhere from ceiling ("no
   unsupported claim") to low ("asserts the evidence lacks X"). That is a source of judge–human disagreement the
   owner will see on the slice. Benchmarks avoid the confound by scoring refusal separately (RAGAS NaN, CRAG
   "missing" = 0, RGB rejection rate, Trust-Score grounded refusals). If abstentions are pooled, an arm with weaker
   retrieval that abstains more can post a *better* judged groundedness delta than `hybrid`.
4. **The paper row is read as a like-for-like baseline.** Table 5 is a dense-only, LlamaIndex, 256-token-chunk,
   2023-embedding setup. Its prose defines Hit@K differently from the script. Its reranker columns re-rank 20
   chunks. A reader who sees lancet's `hybrid` Hits@4 next to voyage-02's 0.4619 or 0.6625 will draw a conclusion
   the setup cannot support unless the caveat sits next to the row (D-103). The arm-delta false-positive risks
   (multiplicity, a liberal bootstrap test) are in the last rubric row above.

extracted from this file and executed at spec time (2026-10-06). Output:**
`QWK=0.8807 (computed) rho=0.8719 kappa95CI=(0.7382…, 0.9545…)` · `ceiling-heavy QWK: value=-0.0526` ·

```python
import importlib.util, random, sys
from lancet_eval.agreement import bootstrap_agreement_ci, quadratic_weighted_kappa, spearman_rank_correlation

# 1. D-113/D-114 agreement path on a toy 20-item slice (owner vs judge, one dimension).
human = [5, 4, 5, 3, 2, 5, 4, 1, 3, 5, 4, 5, 2, 3, 5, 4, 5, 3, 4, 5]
judge = [5, 5, 5, 3, 3, 5, 4, 2, 3, 4, 4, 5, 2, 4, 5, 4, 5, 3, 5, 5]
qwk = quadratic_weighted_kappa(human, judge, min_rating=1, max_rating=5)
rho = spearman_rank_correlation(human, judge)
ci = bootstrap_agreement_ci(human, judge, "kappa", min_rating=1, max_rating=5, seed=42)  # b defaults to 1000
print(f"QWK={qwk.value:.4f} ({qwk.state}) rho={rho.value:.4f} kappa95CI={ci}")
# Critical failure mode 1, made concrete: 18/20 exact agreement at the ceiling gives QWK ~ -0.05.
print("ceiling-heavy QWK:", quadratic_weighted_kappa([5] * 18 + [4, 5], [5] * 18 + [5, 4]))

# 2. Official MultiHop-RAG calculate_metrics (pinned c1c1287), loaded from a local copy.
spec = importlib.util.spec_from_file_location("official", sys.argv[1])
official = importlib.util.module_from_spec(spec); spec.loader.exec_module(official)

def paper_metrics_by_id(ranked_ids, gold_chunk_sets):
    """(PROPOSED) calculate_metrics over chunk IDs. gold_chunk_sets[q][i] = chunk IDs of evidence fact i."""
    h10 = h4 = 0; ap, rr = [], []
    for ranked, gold in zip(ranked_ids, gold_chunk_sets, strict=True):
        found, ap_sum, first = set(), 0.0, None
        for rank, cid in enumerate(ranked[:10], start=1):            # official: retrieved[:11] and rank <= 10
            if any(cid in s for s in gold):                           # relevant = holds ANY gold fact
                new = [i for i, s in enumerate(gold) if cid in s and i not in found]
                first = first or rank; found.update(new)
                ap_sum += len(new) / rank                             # NON-STANDARD: facts first found here / rank
        h10 += first is not None; h4 += first is not None and first <= 4
        ap.append(ap_sum / min(len(gold), 10)); rr.append(1 / first if first else 0)
    n = len(gold_chunk_sets)
    return {"Hits@10": h10 / n, "Hits@4": h4 / n, "MAP@10": sum(ap) / n, "MRR@10": sum(rr) / n}

# 3. Golden vectors: synthetic chunk texts -> the official function on text, ours on IDs.
rng, words, bad, trials, queries = random.Random(42), "alpha beta gamma delta eps zeta eta theta iota kappa lam mu".split(), 0, 0, 0
strip = lambda s: s.replace(" ", "").replace("\n", "")
for _ in range(500):
    chunks = {f"c{i}": " ".join(rng.choice(words) for _ in range(12)) for i in range(40)}
    R, T, G, I = [], [], [], []
    for _ in range(rng.randint(1, 6)):
        facts = []
        for _ in range(rng.randint(2, 4)):
            src = chunks[rng.choice(list(chunks))].split(); s = rng.randint(0, 8)
            facts.append(" ".join(src[s:s + rng.randint(3, 4)]))
        # The 500 sample has no duplicate fact and no fact inside another (checked); keep the generator honest.
        if len({strip(f) for f in facts}) < len(facts) or any(a != b and strip(a) in strip(b) for a in facts for b in facts):
            continue
        ranked = rng.sample(list(chunks), rng.randint(0, 14))
        R.append(ranked); T.append([chunks[c] for c in ranked]); G.append(facts)
        I.append([frozenset(c for c, t in chunks.items() if strip(f) in strip(t)) for f in facts])
    if R:
        a, b = official.calculate_metrics(T, G), paper_metrics_by_id(R, I)
        bad += any(abs(a[m] - b[m]) > 1e-12 for m in a); trials += 1; queries += len(R)
print(f"golden-vector: {trials} trials / {queries} queries evaluated, {bad} with mismatch")
```

**What the golden test proves, and what it does not.** It proves the **arithmetic**: the window, the
non-standard MAP@10 numerator, the `min(len(gold), 10)` divisor and the averaging. It does **not** prove that the
real ID mapping matches the official **text rule**. The synthetic IDs above were built *with* the official rule.
The real `gold_chunks.jsonl` was built by a different matcher (see Pitfall 1). In the committed test, do not
vendor `retrieval_evaluate.py`: its licence has not been checked. Commit precomputed golden vectors instead,
as JSON in `eval/tests/fixtures/` (inputs plus the official outputs, with the commit SHA in the file
header). §4 Core Pattern 4 covers the store cross-check that tests the matching rule.

### Common Pitfalls

1. **"Paper convention" silently computed with a different matching rule.** The official script matches a gold
   fact as a **case-sensitive** substring of **any** retrieved chunk, after stripping `" "` and `"\n"`.
   `gold_chunks.jsonl` came from `classify_evidence_item` (`engine/src/bin/inspect_lancedb.rs:873`), which
   **lowercases** and collapses whitespace (`normalize_ws`, `:805-807`). It also searches **only the chunks of
   the evidence item's mapped `document_id`** (`:882-895`). So a case-only difference, or the same fact text
   in a duplicate article, can split the ID rule from the text rule. Disclose this in the D-102 caveat, and run
   the store cross-check in §4 Core Pattern 4. **Don't reuse** the existing
   `reference_convention_map_at_10` (`metrics.py:566`) or `hits_at_k` (`metrics.py:269`). Both match on
   512-char wire excerpts (`fact_matches_excerpt`, `metrics.py:145`, UNDECIDABLE when truncated). The MAP
   function also **drops facts longer than a chunk from the divisor**, which the official script never does.
2. **Name collision on "MRR@10".** `score.py:346` already reports `mrr_at_k(gold, chunks, k=10)` over the
   **final 8 excerpts**. The paper-convention metrics need distinct dimension names (for example
   `paper_hits_at_4`, `paper_mrr_at_10`) **(PROPOSED)**. Otherwise a reader compares a rank-≤8, excerpt-matched
   number with the paper's rank-≤10 text-matched one.
3. **The new wire fields never reach the journal.** There are four hops, and every one can drop a field silently:
   - The gateway rejects unknown request keys (`dec.DisallowUnknownFields()`, `gateway/main.go:656`). The new
     key must be added to `ragQueryRequestBody` (`:530-539`) and to the `pb.QueryRAGRequest` mapping (`:678-683`).
   - The snapshot goes out through a **hand-written DTO** with a pinned 10-key contract
     (`gateway/internal/sse/dto.go:56-67`, comment `:109-110`, mapping `:111-142`). `buf generate` does not
     touch it.
   - The harness `RetrievalSnapshot` is `extra="ignore"` (`client.py:58`), so an unmodelled field is
     **dropped**.
   - `RunRecord` is `extra="forbid"` (`journal.py:98-101`), so a new field needs a default, or old journals
     stop parsing.

   If the D-100 field is lost at any hop, @10 becomes uncomputable after a paid drive. The D-108 rehearsal must
   assert the field is **present in `journal.jsonl`**, not only in an engine test.
4. **Skipping dense by passing `dense_port: None`.** `fetch_graph_candidates` returns empty when there is no
   dense port (`retrieve.rs:514-516`), because the graph rows are read through `dense_port.fetch_chunks_by_id`
   (`:543`). Gate the mode inside `execute_inner` instead. Otherwise any future `bm25+graph` arm (D-116)
   silently loses its graph list.
5. **`bm25_only` still pays for the query embedding.** The embedding runs in `ExtractGraphContextNode`
   (`engine/src/workflow/nodes/graph_context.rs:72-93`), **before** the `disable_graph_context` early return
   (`:96-105`). D-99 says per-arm latency and cost "show the real cost of each path". Under `bm25_only` that is
   false unless the embedding is skipped. Skipping it is safe only when the graph is also off, because graph
   seeding takes `query_embedding` (`:108-122`). **Open question for the planner.**
6. **The `graph_off_fusion.golden` pin cannot see a regression in the mode switch.** The test
   (`engine/src/retrieval/tests.rs:1466-1476`) calls `fuse_candidates` / `fuse_cross_variant_candidates`
   directly (`:1398-1463`). A switch in `execute_inner` leaves it green whatever the switch does. Add a
   **node-level** pin: `RetrieveHybridNode` with `FakeDenseRetrievalPort` / `FakeBm25RetrievalPort`
   (`engine/src/workflow/ports.rs:404,502`, both carry `call_count`) under the default request. Compare
   `snapshot` and `result_hash` with a recording made **before** the change. Assert call counts per mode
   (dense-only → BM25 count 0; bm25-only → dense count 0).
7. **Alias resolution that rewrites stored labels.** `legacy_records_digest` hashes `rec.graph_arm`
   (`unpark_gates.py:1468-1490`). Mapping `"graph-off"` → `"hybrid"` at parse time therefore changes the digest
   and breaks the legacy-registry lookup, so the drive 1/1b/2 verdicts no longer re-read unchanged (D-101).
   Resolve aliases **at lookup only**. Keep the field name `graph_arm`, and keep historical values byte-for-byte.
8. **The calibration ordering cannot hold with today's `score --judge`.** One call judges, writes the worksheet
   and rewrites `report.json` with judged aggregates (`score.py:476-645`, `:936-1015`). The worksheet is the
   **first 20 cached records of one arm in journal order** (`:959-980`) and carries `cache_key`. See §4 Core
   Pattern 5.
9. **A cap-bound judge stage silently judges a subset.** `derive_judged_slice_size` returns binding `"cap"`
   with `n_cap < judgeable` (`gate.py:398`), and the loop then samples `judged_qids` (`score.py:549-555`).
   D-112 requires **every** judgeable record, so the D-112 stage must **fail closed** when the cap cannot
   cover the judgeable population. It must not sample down.

**2. D-100 pre-truncation ranking: where the graph reorders, and the invariant (Rust + harness).**

Order of operations, read from source:
- `fuse_cross_variant_candidates` (`retrieve.rs:340`) runs `fuse_variant_lists`, then `apply_graph_list`
  (`fusion.rs:235-236`).
- `apply_graph_list` adds the graph contributions and **re-sorts the whole list** (`:313`).

**Recommended order: draw → owner scores → judge.** `cache_key` is computed from the inputs alone
(`judge.py:142-155`), so the slice can be drawn and keyed before any verdict exists. D-112 judges every
judgeable record anyway, so every slice item is judged afterwards. D-113 only requires the scores to be
committed before any aggregate exists.

In this order the verdicts **do not exist** while the owner scores. That matters because hiding `cache_key`
alone would not blind: a worksheet's answer and evidence text is an exact search key into `judge_cache.json`,
which sits in the same run dir. The order also stops judge-error items, which are plausibly the hard ones,
from being filtered out of the slice. Filtering them out would inflate agreement.

If the planner keeps judge-first instead, verdict blinding is **procedural**: the owner must not open
`judge_cache.json`. **In both orders, arm blinding is procedural.** The same text is a search key into
`journal.jsonl`, which holds `graph_arm`. The owner must not open the journal (or, without commit-reveal, the

### Async-First Design

Inherited: the harness is **synchronous** `httpx` plus `connect_sse` (`06.3.4.1-AI-SPEC.md` §4b), and this phase
adds no asyncio. The judge stage is about 1,380 sequential calls (see the cost section below). That is small
enough that concurrency would add rate-limit and spend-accounting risk for no gain: `accumulated_judge_spend`
is checked before each call, as at `score.py:584-586`. Phase-specific mistakes:
- **Engine (tokio):** the D-100 capture must **clone** IDs out of `fused_candidates` before
  `reranker.rerank(fused_candidates).await` moves the `Vec` (`retrieve.rs:357`). Keep the existing
  `cancel.is_cancelled()` checks. A skipped port must not leave an un-awaited future or a timing entry that
  claims work was done. Report `dense_ms` / `bm25_ms` near 0 honestly; don't omit them.
- **Stream vs await:** the drive still drains SSE to `workflow_completed` before journaling (inherited). The
  judge **awaits** a full JSON object and validates it. Nothing in this phase streams to a user.
- **Never overlap stages:** don't run `lancet-eval judge` while the paid drive is still writing the journal. The
  judgeable population must be read from a closed journal, or the D-112 "every judgeable record" claim
  cannot be checked.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

- **Judge prompt frozen at `v1`.** The reasons are in §4 Model Configuration. One trap: `judge_once` always
  sends `JUDGE_SYSTEM_V1` (`judge.py:291`), whatever `prompt_version` it is passed. A TOML-only version bump
  therefore **invalidates the whole cache without changing the prompt**. A real prompt change needs a new
  constant, version-keyed selection, and a commit before any judging (06.3.1 D-49).
- **System/user separation (existing):** the rubric is the system message. The user message is
  `=== QUESTION === / === ANSWER === / === EVIDENCE ===` (`judge.py:292-297`). Evidence is untrusted data
  inside the user turn.
- **Few-shot:** none, and the calibration slice is **never** used as few-shot examples. That would leak owner
  scores into the judge and make the D-114 agreement circular.
- **`max_tokens` explicit:** 400 from the corpus TOML. Build the judge stage on `load_corpus_config`, not on
  `EvalSettings`, whose `judge_max_tokens = 2048` and `judge_model = "openai/gpt-4o-mini"` defaults
  (`config.py:72-74`) would silently change the judge and the spend estimate.

**Decision: P4 (complete case) instead of three pairwise joins.**
- **The problem with pairwise joins:** with four arms, three D-37 joins give three different question sets. A per-arm value and the Δ in the same row would then not reconcile.
- **What P4 gives:** every per-arm mean and every Δ is computed on the same questions, so Δ = mean_X(P4) − mean_hybrid(P4) exactly, and the three comparisons share one n.
- **The cost:** questions where any arm fails ok(r) drop out. Drive 2 lost 3 of 200 records to errors.
- **Reported:** per arm, the count excluded from P4, split into own failure, another arm's failure, and provenance. The size of each pairwise D-37 join is printed next to \|P4\|.
- **Coverage rule (*Proposed*, inside the pre-registration block):** \|P4\| / 308 ≥ 0.80. That is the 0.80 convention of `gate.py:21` and `thresholds.py:157`. Below it, no primary comparison is evaluable and D-110 applies.

**`answer_usable` on P4: one documented deviation.**
- Every P4 record is scored with `metrics.answer_usable` (`metrics.py:121-130`). A usable record with a **blank** answer scores 0 because no line is extracted.
- This record is a NO_EVIDENCE decline. 06.3.4.1's reading excluded it as non-scorable payload (`score.py:326-332`). Excluding declines would flatter the arm that declines most.
- The two readings coincide when no arm has a usable blank-answer record. Drive 2 had 0 such records among its 197 usable ones. The count is printed per arm.
- **Owner confirmation needed at the D-73 checkpoint.** D-111 names "the SC-3 metric of 06.3.4.1". This reading changes the population in two ways (P4 complete case, and a blank answer scored 0). The owner should confirm it still counts as that metric before the pre-registration commit.

### Abstention: one predicate (decision)

```text
is_abstention(r) :=  any(n.typed_code == 1 for n in r.notices)                       # NO_EVIDENCE
                  OR extract_final_answer(r.answer) == "insufficient information"  # D-70 parser + D-72 equality
```

- **Parser.** `extract_final_answer` (`metrics.py:65,71-83`) behaves as follows:
  - the last `Answer:` line wins;
  - `**Answer:**` and `[n]` markers are tolerated;
  - the line is SQuAD-normalised, so `Answer: Insufficient information.` becomes `insufficient information`.

  The equality is the same one D-72 already uses (`null_abstention_correct`, `metrics.py:133-142`).
- **Notice clause.** This is the 06.3-AI-SPEC #5 "notices first" channel. With NO_EVIDENCE present, the runner skips AssemblePrompt and GenerateAnswer (`engine/src/workflow/runner.rs:503-512`). Such a record has a blank answer and no citations, so it counts as an abstention and can never reach the judge.
- **Excluded from the predicate:** the prose fallback of `abstention_outcome` (`metrics.py:537-540`), which matches "insufficient information" or "cannot answer" **anywhere** in the answer. It would count a hedged answer that goes on to commit to `Answer: Yes`. It is reported as a **leak count** instead: records with is_abstention false whose `normalize_ws(answer)` contains either phrase.
- **Inherited rows.** `abstention_on_unanswerable` (06.3, `score.py:1463-1500`) and `null_abstention_correctness` (D-72, `score.py:1501-1530`) stay in the report, labelled **non-governing**.
- **When it is committed.** The predicate is committed as code with tests **before the paid drive**. Once per-arm abstention rates are visible, choosing the predicate would be a forking path.
- **Drive-2 census** (spec time, `eval/runs/2026-10-06-drive2-multihop_rag_diag/journal.jsonl`):
  - the final line was `insufficient information` on 31/90 graph-off and 30/90 graph-on non-null records, and on 9/10 nulls per arm;
  - 0 NO_EVIDENCE notices;
  - 0 prose-only leaks;
  - the only 3 blank answers were `outcome: error` records.

**Decision: the judge does not run on abstaining records or on null questions.** These records are not judged and then excluded; they are never judged.
- **Undefined scores:** an abstention makes no answer claim, so groundedness and faithfulness are undefined for it. RAGAS returns NaN here and CRAG scores "missing" (§1b).
- **No forking path:** verdicts that never exist cannot be pooled into a judged mean later.
- **Matching calibration:** the slice is drawn from the same population, so the judge is calibrated on what it is reported on.
- **Spend:** about a third of the calls are saved. That is about $0.10, so money is not the reason.

**Implementation.**
- `_is_judgeable` (`score.py:141-146`) gains a keyword-only policy argument.
- The **default keeps today's predicate byte for byte** (gold present ∧ citations), so a historical judged run re-scores unchanged.
- The 06.3.5 emit, judge and score paths pass the 06.3.5 policy. That gives one function in one place, which D-112 asks for ("the shared `_is_judgeable` predicate").
- **Key pre-flight:** the stage also refuses to start if `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` is empty. `judge_once` returns an error without calling the API (`judge.py:288-289`), the stage caches that error, and a cached error is never retried (`score.py:581-582`).

**Reading a judged Δ (the selection effect).**
- **The pair set:** the judged Δ (X − hybrid) is computed over the P4 questions where **both** arms produced a judged, non-abstaining answer. It answers "when both arms answer, whose answers are better grounded?". It does not say which arm is better grounded overall.
- **Why that matters:** suppose X abstains more often on hard questions. Those questions then leave the pair set for both arms, so the pair set is easier than P4.
- **Columns on every judged Δ row:**
  - n_pairs;
  - the questions dropped because only X abstained, only `hybrid` abstained, or both abstained;
  - judge errors per arm;
  - the paired abstention-rate Δ for the same arm.
- **Flag:** when that abstention-rate Δ has a 95% CI that excludes 0, the row reads **"abstention differs: conditional on both arms answering; not an arm effect on grounding"**.
- **Per-arm judged means:** these are over each arm's own J_a and are labelled "different question sets; compare arms only through the paired Δ".
- **No inference:** judged deltas are secondaries (D-111), with no p-value.

### Judge rubric: keep v1 (decision)

  - The abstention problem is solved by the population rule above, not by the prompt.
  - A prompt change would bump the version and invalidate every cache key, and it would have to be committed before any judging (06.3.1 D-49). Nothing here needs that.

- **Evidence meta-statements** ("Evidence [2] … do not directly address the comparison") are claims about what the evidence says, and v1 grades them as claims.

- **Never in the judge's input:** the gold answer, the arm, the question type or the question ID. The user turn is QUESTION / ANSWER / EVIDENCE only (`judge.py:291-296`).

### Paper-convention metrics (D-102), exactly

- **Semantics.** `calculate_metrics` at `c1c1287`, as given in the verified facts and §3.
  - Walk the first 10 ranked items. The script iterates `[:11]` and keeps rank ≤ 10.
  - An item is relevant if it holds **any** gold fact.
  - Hits@k: some relevant item sits at rank ≤ k.
  - MRR@10: 1 / the first relevant rank.
  - MAP@10: Σ over relevant ranks r of (gold facts **first** found at r) / r, divided by min(\|gold\|, 10).
  - All four are averaged over the population.

  The implementation is `paper_metrics_by_id` (§3 Entry Point), with golden vectors committed as JSON (§3).
- **Gold sets.** Each `(question_id, evidence_index)` row of `gold_chunks.jsonl` becomes `frozenset(chunk_ids)`. G is defined so that every fact in H_G is `in_chunk` (D-63), so no gold set there is empty. The empty-set rule (it counts in the divisor and never matches) is kept anyway for correctness.
- **Ranking input.** `[c.chunk_id for c in snapshot.pre_truncation_ranking]`, already in `fused_rank` order. Its first 8 equal `retrieved_chunks` (#1). A record without a valid ranking fails ok(r) and so falls outside P4.
- **Population.** The headline is P4. The paper averages over all non-null queries of its 2,556-question set. G-restriction removes questions with a fact split across chunks or missing from the index, which **raises** lancet's figures relative to a full-population reading. The caveat (#14) says so.
- **Errored and partial records (06.3.1 D-34): excluded, not counted as misses.**
  - **Same population as the primary test:** the paired Δ the primary is tested on runs on P4, so per-arm values and Δs reconcile.
  - **Binding convention:** D-34 exclusion is the harness's binding rule.
  - **Errors stay visible:** they are gated per arm (D-109) and counted per arm.
  - **Script-faithful sensitivity line (paper metrics only):** every H_G question per arm. A record without a valid ranking scores as an empty retrieval list (a miss), and the denominator is 308. This line is labelled and never decides anything.

  - Percentile bootstrap (`bootstrap_mean_ci`, B = 10 000, seed 42) for MRR and MAP per arm.

What the plan commits, verbatim in substance:

1. **Primaries.**
   - `paper_hits_at_4`, for retrieval, under the ID rule.
   - `answer_usable`, for answers: 06.3.4.1's SC-3 metric, on P4.

   Both are 0/1 per question.
2. **Comparisons.** dense-only − hybrid, bm25-only − hybrid and hybrid+graph − hybrid, on P4, two-sided.
3. **Family.** There are **two families, one per primary, with m = 3 each**. That is D-111's own wording: "across the three arm-vs-`hybrid` paired deltas **within each primary**". Each family holds FWER ≤ 0.05. Across both primaries the familywise error is ≤ 0.10 (Bonferroni bound), which D-111's choice accepts. The report states it.
4. **Test.** A paired sign-flip permutation test, **computed exactly**.
   - Both primaries are 0/1, so each per-question difference d is −1, 0 or +1. Zeros do not change under a flip.
   - Over the n_d discordant pairs, the statistic S = Σd has the null distribution S* = 2K − n_d, with K ~ Binomial(n_d, ½).
   - p = P(\|S*\| ≥ \|S_obs\|), computed in integer arithmetic (`math.comb` and an exact fraction). There is no Monte Carlo error and no seed. If n_d = 0, then p = 1. This is the exact McNemar test.
   - **Why not Monte Carlo flips:** at α/3 = 0.0167, 10 000 random flips carry a Monte Carlo SE of about 0.0013. That is enough to flip a Holm decision near the line.
   - A Monte Carlo version (10 000 flips, seed 42, integer sums, p = (1 + #{\|S*\| ≥ \|S_obs\|}) / (1 + 10 000)) is kept **only** as a pytest golden cross-check.
   - **On §1b's sign-test caution:** Smucker et al. advise against the sign test for continuous per-topic scores, because it discards magnitudes. For 0/1 scores, the permutation test and the sign test on discordant pairs are the same test, so nothing is discarded.
5. **Holm.**
   - Sort the three p ascending.
   - Reject H(1) if p(1) ≤ 0.05/3; then H(2) if p(2) ≤ 0.05/2; then H(3) if p(3) ≤ 0.05.
   - Stop at the first non-rejection.
   - Report the adjusted p̃(i) = max over j ≤ i of min(1, (4 − j)·p(j)).
   - **Only a Holm rejection may be called "significant".**
6. **Estimation.** The paired Δ gets a percentile bootstrap 95% CI (`bootstrap_mean_ci`, B = 10 000, seed 42; D-38), and per-arm rates get Wilson 95% intervals. These CIs are **unadjusted**. A CI that excludes 0 without a Holm rejection is reported as exactly that.
7. **Coverage.** If \|P4\| / 308 < 0.80, all three comparisons read "not evaluable: coverage", and D-110 applies.
8. **Robustness (`paper_hits_at_4` only).** Holm is re-run on the text-rule figures (#5). A decision that differs reads "not robust to the matching rule", with no directional claim.
9. **Secondaries, with no inferential claim:**
   - `paper_hits_at_10`, `paper_mrr_at_10`, `paper_map_at_10`;
   - recall@4 and precision@4;
   - the D-70 secondaries;
   - the abstention rates;
   - both judged dimensions;
   - latency and cost;
   - every D-40 stratum, including the primaries' strata.
10. **Direction.** Results are published whichever direction they take (D-49). A worse arm is a result, not a gate miss (D-110).

**Why this order.**
- **Verdict blinding is structural.** Verdicts **do not exist** while the owner scores. Hiding `cache_key` would not be enough, because a row's answer and evidence text is an exact search key into `judge_cache.json`.
- **No filtering of judge errors.** The draw cannot drop judge-error items, which are plausibly the hard ones.
- **No early aggregates.** The only stage that creates verdicts refuses to start until the scores are committed. No judged aggregate or per-arm judged Δ can exist before them (D-113).
- **Arm blinding stays procedural.** The same text is a search key into `journal.jsonl`, which holds `graph_arm`. The owner does not open the journal or K until step 5.
- **What commit-reveal adds.** K stays out of accidental view, and the mapping is provably fixed before scoring.

**Draw (PROPOSED `calibration.py`).**
- **Pool, per arm:** records with q ∈ P4 and judgeable₀₆₃₅(r), so non-null, non-abstaining, ok(r) and cited.
  - **Abstentions are not eligible.** They are not in the judged population. They are also ceiling items, and §1b shows ceiling items either break QWK or flatter it.
- **Seed:** `CALIBRATION_DRAW_SEED = 42`, the repo convention (`stats.py:8`, corpus `sample_seed`). It is committed before the drive, so it cannot be chosen once the records exist. The draw uses a single `random.Random(seed)` stream.
- **Quota per arm:** 2 comparison, 2 inference and 1 temporal, so the slice is 8/8/4. Temporal gets one per arm because it is scarce among non-abstaining answers (see above).
- **Draw order:**
  - arms in registry order: dense-only, bm25-only, hybrid, hybrid+graph;
  - within an arm: comparison, then inference, then temporal;
  - candidates sorted by question_id;
  - `rng.choice` among candidates whose question_id and cache_key are not yet drawn. Questions are distinct across the whole slice, so the owner never compares two arms' answers to the same question.
- **Empty cell:** fill it from the same arm's other types, in the order comparison → inference → temporal, and record the substitution in K. Refuse if an arm has fewer than 5 eligible items.
- **Finish:** shuffle the 20 rows with the same stream and assign `S01`..`S20`.

- **Header:** corpus, judge_prompt_version, judge_model, seed, emitted_at_sha, d114_floor, key_sha256.
- **Rubric block, printed verbatim from `JUDGE_SYSTEM_V1`:**

- **Rows** (`CalibrationWorksheetRow`, §4b, `extra="forbid"`): `slice_id`, `question`, `answer`, `evidence`, `human_groundedness`, `human_faithfulness`, `notes`.

### D-114 trust floor. *Proposed; commit in `thresholds.py` before the paid held-out drive.*

```python

# exists (AI-SPEC §5 "D-114 trust floor"). Applied separately to each judged dimension, to the QWK
# point estimate on the owner-scored slice, at or above; the 95% CI is disclosed, never decisive.
# Value: continuity with gate.py AGREEMENT_TARGET (0.70); inside Landis & Koch's "substantial" band
# and above McHugh's 0.60 "inadequate" line. Those bands were proposed for unweighted kappa, so using
# them for QWK is a convention, not a derivation (AI-SPEC §1b).
JUDGE_QWK_TRUST_FLOOR: float = 0.70
# 06.3.5 D-113: fewer scored pairs than this (after judge-error attrition) -> "uncalibrated".
CALIBRATION_MIN_SCORED_PAIRS: int = 16
# 06.3.5 D-113: seed of the 20-item slice draw (one random.Random stream).
CALIBRATION_DRAW_SEED: int = 42
```

| Condition, per dimension, checked in this order | Label |
|---|---|
| QWK state is `undefined_expected_agreement` (`agreement.py:119-121`) | **uncalibrated: QWK undefined** |
| Fewer than 16 scored pairs | **uncalibrated: slice attrition** |
| QWK point estimate < 0.70 | **uncalibrated** |
| QWK point estimate ≥ 0.70 | **calibrated (n, QWK, 95% CI printed)** |

Only a **calibrated** dimension may drive a 06.3.6 default decision. Every judged number is published whatever its label (D-114).

**Why 0.70, honest about n = 20.**
- **Continuity.** The report already carries 0.70 (`gate.py:25`), and one agreement number avoids two thresholds in one report.
- **Bands.** 0.70 sits inside Landis & Koch's "substantial" band (0.61–0.80) and clears McHugh's "below 0.60 is inadequate". These are band conventions for unweighted kappa (§1b). No published QWK exists for a 1–5 RAG groundedness judge to anchor the value instead.
- **Noise.** The decision statistic is a point estimate on 20 items. **For a judge whose long-run QWK sits at the floor, the label is close to a coin flip.** The table below shows how often a judge is labelled calibrated at each candidate floor.

  How the figures were produced: **[computed]** by the eval-planner's extension of §1b's simulation, `research/qwk_n20_sim_floor.py` with its `.out` in this phase folder, run through the repo's `agreement.py`. It keeps §1b's human marginals. The judge is an independent uniform 1–5 draw with probability r, and otherwise the human score plus medium noise. Each row is 2,000 slices of n = 20. The data is synthetic, not repo data.

  | Long-run QWK | Labelled calibrated at floor 0.70 | at floor 0.60 |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.84 | 0.92 | 0.99 |
  | 0.75 | 0.64 | 0.82 |
  | 0.66 | 0.40 | 0.64 |
  | 0.57 | 0.24 | 0.46 |
  | 0.49 | 0.12 | 0.27 |

  §1b's noise-only model gives the same picture: a judge at 0.764 is labelled calibrated 72% of the time at 0.70.
- **Trade.**
  - **At 0.60:** a judge below McHugh's adequacy line (≈ 0.57) is labelled calibrated about half the time.
  - **At 0.70:** that falls to about a quarter. The cost is that a decent judge (≈ 0.75) is labelled uncalibrated about a third of the time.
  - **The costs are asymmetric.** "Uncalibrated" only bars a secondary from a 06.3.6 decision, while "calibrated" lets it drive one. 0.70 takes the conservative side.
- **What the labels mean.**
  - **Calibrated:** this slice did not show agreement below the floor.
  - **Uncalibrated:** not *shown* trustworthy. It does **not** mean the judge was shown untrustworthy (§1b).
  - Neither label says more about long-run agreement than the printed CI does.

**Relation to the existing gate (no silent disagreement).**
- **The legacy state is still printed.** `calibration_state` (`score.py:868-894`) is still computed: QWK ≥ `AGREEMENT_TARGET` **and** Spearman ≥ `AGREEMENT_TARGET`, with b = 1000 CIs. The 06.3.5 report prints it labelled **"legacy (06.3), non-governing"**.
- **D-114 governs every use.**
- **Divergence is spelled out.** Whenever the two differ for a dimension, the report prints one line: "legacy calibration_state and D-114 disagree for <dimension>; D-114 governs." Examples: QWK ≥ 0.70 with Spearman < 0.70; slice attrition with both statistics ≥ 0.70.
- **No edits to the old gate.** `gate.py` is not edited.

## 7. Production Monitoring

> This is an offline eval phase, with no production traffic and no on-call. "Monitoring" means what each stage's journal, cache and stage record must show. `06.3.4.1-AI-SPEC.md` §7 is inherited.

**Tracing Tool:** inherited, with no new SDK:
- the `lancet-eval` journal: per-record `snapshot` (now with `retrieval_mode` and the D-100 ranking), `node_timings`, `workflow_meta`, `notices` and `correlation_id`;
- the Phase 6.2 OpenTelemetry → Collector → Jaeger path, joined through `X-Lancet-Correlation-ID`;
- `judge_cache.json` as the audit trail for every verdict, keyed by `cache_key`.

The Arize Phoenix default is **overridden**: the §2 stack is locked, and 06.3-AI-SPEC §5 already rejected Phoenix as a redundant second OTel backend.

**Key Metrics to Track:**
1. **Rehearsal:**
   - the §5 #1 provenance checks per record;
   - the D-100 field and the mode echo, present **in `journal.jsonl`**;
   - judge latency per call, if the optional rehearsal judge sample runs; it sets the judge stage's wall-clock estimate for its D-86 text.
2. **Drive**, per arm and pooled:
   - completeness;
   - errors by class under `--retries 0`;
   - provenance failures;
   - NO_EVIDENCE count;
   - RetrieveHybrid p95;
   - spend against the cap;
   - engine PID and start time before and after.
3. **Judge stage:**
   - calls, cache hits, and errors per arm;
   - the consecutive-error run;
   - spend against the cap;
   - wall-clock.
4. **Calibration:**
   - the emit, scores, reveal and judge commit SHAs, in order;
   - slice attrition.

**Alert Thresholds:** there is no pager. The triggers are the §6 stop conditions:
- a cap stop;
- a provenance failure;
- a judge error rate > 0.05 after ≥ 50 calls, or 5 consecutive errors;
- \|P4\| / 308 < 0.80;
- an ordering-gate refusal;
- an engine restart mid-drive.

**Smart Sampling Strategy.** The operator reads these rows before the run-of-record commit. "Seeded" means seed 42.
- **All of:**
  - every provenance failure;
  - every P4 exclusion;
  - every matching-rule disagreement;
  - every judge error;
  - every slice item where \|judge − human\| ≥ 2 on either dimension;
  - every `hallucinated_on_null` record;
  - every leak row.
- **5 seeded per comparison arm:**
  - questions where the arm and `hybrid` disagree on `paper_hits_at_4` but not on `answer_usable`, and the reverse;
  - pairs where only one arm abstained.
- **3 seeded P4 rows per arm.**
