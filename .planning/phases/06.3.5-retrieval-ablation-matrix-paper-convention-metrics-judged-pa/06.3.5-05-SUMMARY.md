# Phase 06.3.5 Plan 05: Four-arm registry, registry-driven requests and the seeded rotation Summary

**D-101 four-arm `ARM_REGISTRY` that drives the corpus `[arms]`, `probe --arm`, the request flags and the provenance check, plus a pure seeded balanced rotation over the committed split with heldout/rehearsal refusals and header provenance.**

## Performance

- **Duration:** about 45 min
- **Tasks:** 3 (each with a RED test commit and a GREEN feat commit)
- **Files modified:** 9 (2 new, 7 modified), all under `eval/`

## Accomplishments

- `arms.py` is the single source of arm truth: four rows (`dense-only`, `bm25-only`, `hybrid`, `hybrid+graph`), read-only aliases `graph-off` / `graph-on`, `request_fields` that reproduces drive 1/1b/2 bodies exactly for the legacy labels and adds `retrieval_mode` plus `include_pre_truncation_ranking: true` for canonical labels.
- Corpus configs cannot name an unknown arm; `[split]`, `[judge] protocol` and `[preflight]` are parsed with legacy defaults, and the real heldout / rehearsal TOMLs expose the expected values.
- The companion invariant `test_every_registry_arm_round_trips_request_to_provenance` iterates `ARM_REGISTRY`: body keys equal `request_fields`, a mocked stream echoes the mode, `mode_provenance_failures` returns `[]`, and a swapped echo fails naming the arm.
- `plan_work_units` gives 88/88/88/87 first-arm and per-position counts over the 351 held-out questions, is independent of input order, and `--resume` / `--limit` operate on the same rotated list.
- `drive` refuses a `heldout` corpus unless its IDs equal the split's 308 + 43 and a `rehearsal` corpus that holds any dev or held-out ID, before any journal I/O or request. Split drive headers carry `order_seed` and `split_sha256`; legacy headers are unchanged.
