### What drives the MISS (measured from the journal and the table)

- **Step change in answer format.** In drive order, the first 22 graph-off successes all carry `Answer: <short>` at the start of a line. From the 23rd graph-off success on (journal record index 53, question `mhr-306f9da5e9e7`, `started_at_ms` 1790752343881 = 2026-09-30T07:12:23Z, about 5 minutes into the drive), **0 of the remaining 74 graph-off successes do** (74 includes the null questions; 68 of them are in G). The graph-on arm changes at the same place. Prompt tokens (median 1985 before, 1982 after), completion tokens (278 before, 257 after) and error rates do not move; only the position of the words `Answer:` does. 62 of the 74 late answers do contain `Answer: <short>` at the end of the same line, as in `... The evidence is consistent and directly supports the identification of ChatGPT. Answer: ChatGPT`. The other 12 have no `Answer:` at all.
- **Consequence.** `extract_final_answer` reads only a line-start `Answer:`, so those 62 read as `final_answer_missing`. Of the 65 graph-off successes in G whose retrieval had the gold chunk in the vector top 4 (column c yes), 12 are usable, **51 are not usable AND final_answer_missing**, and 2 are not usable with the line present (wrong answer).
- **Cause is not established.** The format change is abrupt, so it looks like something changed in the model's output or its provider route during the drive, not a question effect. No provider field is journaled and this plan did not investigate it. Earlier journals show a mix rather than a cliff: 06.3.4.1-26's confirmation (both arms) had 66 of 113 answers with a line-start `Answer:` and 111 of 113 with `Answer:` anywhere.
- **Counterfactual (post hoc, NOT the gated reading, and not a PASS):** counting the last `Answer:` anywhere in the text, 49 of 88 graph-off G answers contain the gold (0.5568, Wilson 95% CI [0.4528, 0.6561]); 12 have no `Answer:` at all. Split by gold type: entity gold 25/32 (0.7812, CI [0.6124, 0.8898]) against 6/32 committed; binary gold 24/56 (0.4286, CI [0.3077, 0.5586]) against 9/56 committed, which is **below** the binary constant-Yes baseline of 0.661. The count was made after seeing the data with a rule that is not the committed extractor, so it is not used for the SC-3 verdict (D-73 keeps the literals fixed before data). It is shown only to size the line-start effect; its overall CI straddles the 0.512 floor and it does not establish that the vector baseline would pass.
- **Retrieval is not the bottleneck on this sample:** column (a) yes 90/90, (b) yes 90/90, (c) yes 65/90 answerable.
