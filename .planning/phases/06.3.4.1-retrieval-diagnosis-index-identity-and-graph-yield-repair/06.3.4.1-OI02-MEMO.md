# OI-02 Memo: RetrieveHybrid Latency Growth — Investigation Status (2026-09-26)

This memo is written for someone who was not in the room for any of this investigation. It
summarizes what OI-02 is, everything plan 06.3.4.1-07 tried to reproduce it, where that
investigation stands now, and what a future paid drive (06.3.4.1-11/-12) needs to capture so
OI-02 can finally be diagnosed live. Full detail, evidence, and exact numbers live in
`06.3.4.1-OI02-PROFILE.md`; this memo is the entry point, not a replacement for it.

---

## PAST SITUATION

### What OI-02 is

During the Phase 06.3.4 "corrected re-drive" (2026-09-09/10), a live paid drive against the
`multihop_rag` corpus recorded 658 work units before the operator halted it. Partway through,
the `RetrieveHybrid` retrieval node's latency escalated steadily: its median went from
**4,910.5 ms** on the first 50-record slice to **28,960.0 ms** on the seventh 50-record slice —
a **5.9x** growth over the first 350 records the journal has complete node timings for (records
past roughly ordinal 350 are increasingly censored: no completed `RetrieveHybrid` timing at all).
The drive was halted specifically because of this escalation (recorded elsewhere as "7.4s to
>30s"). The full 658-record journal spans about 24.6 hours of wall clock, of which roughly 16
hours are idle gaps between records (retries, pauses) — active processing time across the whole
658-record run was roughly 8.7 hours.

Every other workflow node (`AssemblePrompt`, a pure-CPU node with no network or database call;
`ExtractGraphContext`; `GenerateAnswer`) also grew over the same window, by smaller multiples
(1.28x–5.02x). A single node growing would point at that node specifically; every node growing
together is one of the strongest pieces of evidence that whatever mechanism is responsible is
process-wide, not local to retrieval.

OI-02 is the open engineering item to find that mechanism and fix it at the root — D-64 requires
this before any further paid drive, and specifically forbids treating a restart, a raised
timeout budget, or a retry-count change as a fix.

### What came before plan 07

Plan 06.3.4.1-03 ran three independent in-process soak passes against the live store with the
engine's own retrieval code, no gateway, no real provider (`retrieval_soak`, single-threaded,
provider ports replaced by deterministic in-bin stand-ins):
- a **release-build** soak, 300 iterations per arm across five arm variants;
- a **debug-build** soak, 300 iterations, matching production's own (later-established) debug
  launch;
- a **2.5-hour, production-paced debug soak** (474 iterations, `--pace-ms 17656
  --duration-secs 9000`) — the one long-wall-clock-duration test this soak design could offer,
  approximating production's per-question cadence with an idle delay between iterations.

None of the three reproduced meaningful `RetrieveHybrid` growth. The 2.5-hour paced run did find
one counter that grows continuously rather than plateauing — the Lance session cache
(`session_size_bytes`/`session_approx_num_items`, +1.9%/+10.5% over the run) — but that growth
did not correlate with retrieval latency, which stayed flat over the same window. The checkpoint
resolution was **diagnose-in-drive-1**: no accumulator class was ever named, so neither
"fix-named-class" nor "mimalloc" was available as an option, and the soak design (in-process,
provider-free, single-threaded, sub-3-minute-to-2.5-hour scale) had reached the ceiling of what
it could rule in or out.

### What plan 07 did

Plan 07 replaced the original (soak-based) plan 07 with a full-stack replay design specifically
because the soak could never exercise the real gRPC/tokio per-request service path, the real
gateway, or a real provider.

**Task 1 — zero-cost forensics.** Established from Prometheus counters, Loki startup lines, and
PowerShell command history (no re-run of the drive needed): the engine was essentially fresh at
record 1 (`fresh_process: true`, only 2 prior queries — one health-check ping plus one warm-up);
the collector was reachable throughout the drive (zero export gaps); the build was **debug**, not
release (every launch command in history omits `--release`); and the drive ran on an
**uncommitted** code state that predates commit `33e774bd` (the "drive-era" commit, which later
committed the same `--retries` flag and config the drive actually used). `config_toml_at_drive_time`
could not be resolved — two candidate configs from before/after the drive disagree on several
timeout values, and no log line pins down which was live. Ranked hypotheses were written from
this evidence, in order: (1) process-wide degradation on the real per-request service path
(never soak-testable — the strongest candidate), (2) debug-build-specific degradation
compounding hypothesis 1, (3) Lance session cache growth (carried forward from plan 03, a watch
item, not ruled out), (4) telemetry/logging-exporter load under a reachable collector with no
level filter (never soak-tested), (5) abandoned CPU work outliving a timed-out future (soak
already weakened this), (6) gateway/transport backpressure (already ruled out for this episode
by a zero-cost journal analysis in plan 03).

**Task 2 — replay tooling, then the primary replay.** Built a loopback-only provider stub
(`provider_stub.py`), replay orchestration (`scripts/oi02-replay.ps1`) that stands up isolated
store/database copies, launches the real engine and gateway, and tears everything down with
before/after proof that the live store and schema were untouched, and analysis tooling
(`oi02.py`'s `forensics`, `replay-summary`, `check-arm` subcommands). Ran a 2-question smoke,
then the **primary** replay (350 records, HEAD code, reconciled store, stub provider). Two
significant fidelity/tooling bugs surfaced and were fixed before results could be trusted:
- **`primary`'s own embedding vectors were 100% hash-fallback**, not the intended per-question
  real embeddings, because a caller-side wiring step (`retrieval_soak --export-embeddings` plus
  matching each replay question to production's real top-ranked chunk) had never actually been
  built. Every graph-on record got `GRAPH_ABLATION`/`GRAPH_UNAVAILABLE` — no real graph
  traversal ever ran. This made `primary` an invalid test of the graph path, so the checkpoint at
  Task 3 was resolved **rerun-primary**: the wiring was built, and **`primary-r2`** re-ran with
  real embeddings (172/175 questions matched to production's real vectors, 98.3%).
- **A classifier bug**: `classify_m2`'s "not grows" branch compared against a fixed slice-3
  instead of literally the plan's own "last full slice" (slice-6 at this 350-record/7-slice
  scale) — at this scale these are different slices, and the bug had been silently
  mis-classifying "not grows" as passing when the real last-slice ratio was actually just over
  the ceiling. Found and fixed (test-first); both `primary` and `primary-r2` reclassified from
  "not grows" to **ambiguous** (a labeling correction only — routing was unaffected, since the
  plan's routing table treats "not grows" and "ambiguous" identically).
- **`primary-r2`'s M1 formula bug**: `replay_summary` always used the reconciled-store ratio
  formula, even for pre-reconcile-store arms, which need a direct comparison instead. Found and
  fixed (test-first) before any Route B arm ran.

`primary-r2` (real embeddings, 350 records, HEAD code, reconciled store): **M1 not met** (ratio
5.34 against the required 8.48–33.91 band) and **M2 ambiguous** (RetrieveHybrid moved only
1.02x–1.21x across slices, nowhere near the 5.9x production growth). Real graph seeding (109/175
graph-on records got genuine unflagged traversal, vs. 0/175 on the invalid first primary) did
not change the outcome — ruling out "random vectors suppressed the growth mechanism" as an
explanation.

**Task 3 — checkpoint.** M2 ambiguous (whatever M1 reads) routes to **Route B**: start with the
whole stack at the drive-era commit, because growth was not reproduced at HEAD.

**Task 4 — Route B bisection.** Three free arms, each changing exactly one factor from its
reference arm, in a dedicated git worktree at the drive-era commit (`33e774bd`) with the
pre-reconcile store copy (the actual store production ran against):
- **`drive-era`** (150 records): the drive-era commit's own engine, gateway, and harness,
  against the pre-reconcile store — **not grows**.
- **`drive-era-prewarm`** (150 records, +1 warm-up question matching the established 2
  prior-query count): **not grows**.
- **`paced`** (350 records, stub response latency paced to production's own growing per-slice
  medians instead of a flat delay — testing whether a fixed, instant stub response was itself
  masking a mechanism): **ambiguous at full 350-record scale** (RetrieveHybrid moved 1.29x
  end-to-end, nowhere near 5.9x) — under the plan's own "extend once, then count as not
  reproduced" rule, this is a final result, not one requiring a further extension.
- The journaled real-variant check (query reformulation) needed no new arm: reformulation never
  fires anywhere — production, `primary-r2`, or any replay arm — so there was no variant set to
  diverge.

**Task 5 — checkpoint, paid replay.** With Route B's three free factors flat, the user
authorized one paid replay (hard cap $0.25) to test the two factors no free stub can supply:
real provider round-trip latency and real embedding-call computation. **`paid-drive-era`**
(350 records, same drive-era whole-stack environment, real OpenRouter key and endpoints, no
warm-up): **M1 not met** (0.202x of the fresh-process reference) and **M2 not grows**
(RetrieveHybrid moved 1.14x by slice-3 and 1.14x by the last slice, window delta 143ms — both
under their respective growth/flatness ceilings). Tracked spend $0.1143
(the harness's own real-time cap-enforcement formula); a real, unrelated engine validation
failure on 66/350 records means true spend may be somewhat higher (worst-case estimate ~$0.19,
see below) — still comfortably under the $0.25 cap.

## CURRENT SITUATION

**Growth and the starting gap were not reproduced by any arm, free or paid.**

Ruled out, across free and paid arms:
- the code and store at the drive-era commit (whole-stack `drive-era` arm);
- process warm-up state (`drive-era-prewarm`);
- fixed vs. production-paced provider response latency (`paced`);
- real provider round-trip latency and real embedding computation (`paid-drive-era`);
- run length up to ~350–474 records / up to 2.5 hours of paced wall clock (every soak and
  replay arm tested within this range stayed flat);
- query-reformulation variance (never fires, anywhere);
- store mutation during the *original* 06.3.4 drive specifically (the store's newest version
  predates the drive by a week — confirmed static throughout that drive; this does not cover a
  *future* drive, which should still be monitored for this independently).

**The one clue every arm agrees on:** every replay's first-slice RetrieveHybrid median lands at
roughly **0.2x** of production's own first-slice median (`drive-era` 0.210x, `drive-era-prewarm`
0.198x, `paced` 0.216x, `paid-drive-era` 0.202x) — a narrow, consistent band, regardless of
whether the provider is real or stubbed. Production was already about 5x slower than any replay
**from the very first query**, before any growth had a chance to accumulate. This means the
starting-gap and the growth are probably related to the same underlying difference between the
replay environment and the actual drive-time environment — something present from record 0, not
something that only develops over time. Since none of code, store, warm-up, or provider realism
explains it, what's left is the actual machine and process state present during the real drive,
which none of these replays can recreate.

**That state is no longer reconstructable.** Jaeger is in-memory and has been restarted since;
Prometheus's retention window for the 06.3.4 drive period was already partially expired before
this plan started (the surviving raw samples are preserved in `data/oi02-evidence/`, but that is
metrics, not process/thread/memory state). There is no way to inspect what the actual machine and
engine process looked like at 2026-09-09T21:05Z.

**Not yet tested — candidate factors for a live drive to watch, not for another replay:**
- **Long-lived engine process / heap state.** Every replay in this plan restarts a fresh engine
  process for ~150–350 records. Production's engine process may have been up for much longer,
  serving traffic (or sitting idle) before the drive started, or the drive itself may need to
  run past ~350–474 records at production's own pace before a slow-building degradation (heap
  fragmentation, allocator bookkeeping under the debug build specifically, connection/handle
  accumulation) becomes visible. No replay in this plan ran long enough or came from an
  old-enough process to test this.
- **Full tracing/observability load under sustained real request volume.** `paid-drive-era` and
  every Route B arm ran with the observability stack up and reachable (matching production), but
  only for 150–350 records over roughly an hour — the same combination plan 03's ranked
  hypothesis 4 flagged as never having been tested under sustained load, and it still hasn't been
  tested at anything close to production's ~24-hour, 658-record scale.
- **Store mutations during a live drive.** Confirmed NOT the cause for the *original* 06.3.4
  drive (store was static). A future drive should still confirm this itself — a live drive that
  concurrently ingests or debounces while querying is a genuinely different condition from every
  replay in this plan, which ran retrieval-only against a static copy.
- **Concurrency.** Every replay and every soak ran `--workers 1`, matching production's own
  single-worker drive — so concurrency was never a variable in the *episode this plan is trying
  to explain*, and is not the missing piece for that specific episode. It remains open for
  production load in general (a live service typically handles concurrent requests), which is a
  different question from OI-02's specific growth episode.
- **Lance session cache growth (class 3, still a watch item).** Plan 03's 2.5-hour paced soak
  found continuous (though decelerating) session-cache growth uncorrelated with its own flat
  retrieval latency, at a scale of ~8,400 items / ~17.65 MB. None of plan 07's replays ran long
  enough or touched enough distinct entities to test whether a much larger footprint (production
  touches far more distinct documents/chunks across 658 records) crosses a size threshold this
  soak's scale never reached. The counters to watch
  (`session_approx_num_items`/`session_size_bytes`) are already logged by the `request_process_state`
  instrumentation this plan added to `service.rs` — no new engine work needed to observe this in
  drive 1.

### Incidental findings, not part of OI-02

- **A real engine-level bug, unrelated to latency**: 66 of 350 `paid-drive-era` records failed
  with `"ModelOnly answer basis is not supported on Phase 03 QueryRAG path"` — the model
  sometimes answers without citing any retrieved evidence, and this specific query path rejects
  that outright. This validation is identical at HEAD and at the drive-era commit (no diff), so
  it is a pre-existing, real-provider-only failure mode (roughly 1 in 5 queries), not something
  introduced or removed by any code change in this plan. Logged to `.planning/WINDOWS.md` as an
  open, out-of-scope item — D-64 forbids conflating it with the OI-02 fix.
- **A PowerShell tooling bug**, found and fixed (`d50694dc`): the harness's own benign
  retry-warning log line was being misread by `scripts/oi02-replay.ps1` as a script-terminating
  error under a specific nested-redirection interaction, aborting the replay's own summary step
  even when the underlying 350-record run had completed successfully. Fixed and verified via a
  standalone three-case repro; does not affect any result already recorded in this memo or the
  profile (the affected run's data was recovered and validated by hand before the fix landed).
- **A launcher argument-quoting bug**, found and fixed (`79406d2d`): a comma-bearing stub-delay
  schedule value was being re-parsed as a PowerShell array literal instead of a single string.
  Fixed before the `paced` arm ran; no results were affected.

## CARRY-FORWARD (for paid drive 1, 06.3.4.1-11/-12)

**Risk.** D-64 requires a non-decaying engine before any further paid drive; OI-02 is not fixed.
Drive 1 will run on an unfixed engine. Budget pass A (06.3.4.1-11, D-66) derives its timeout
budgets from drive 1's own measurements — if `RetrieveHybrid` decays the same way it did in
06.3.4, drive 1 is likely to hit its own decay/flatness gate the same way the original 06.3.4
drive did, and budget derivation may not be usable until OI-02 is actually fixed.

**What drive 1 must capture so OI-02 can be diagnosed live** (all of this already exists and
needs no new engine work — it was built specifically for this purpose across plans 03 and 07):
- **Keep the observability stack running and reachable for the entire drive**, and **do not let
  Prometheus's or Loki's retention windows expire before this data is pulled**. This plan lost
  most of the original 06.3.4 window (Jaeger traces gone, most of the Prometheus window expired)
  before it could be used — that must not happen again. Export the relevant Prometheus range and
  Loki logs to `data/oi02-evidence/` (or equivalent) as soon as the drive ends, not weeks later.
- **The `request_process_state` engine event** (`engine/src/service.rs`, added in plan 07's
  Task 2): emits `request_ordinal`, `process_uptime_ms`, `alive_tasks`, `global_queue_depth`,
  `session_approx_num_items` on every request, and `session_size_bytes` every 50th request.
  Already live at HEAD — nothing to build, just don't remove it.
- **`retrieve_hybrid_substages` P1 tracing** (from plan 03's Task 1): sub-stage timings
  (`open_table_ms`, `checkout_ms`, `dense_ms`, `bm25_ms`, `fusion_ms`) inside `RetrieveHybrid`
  itself — already live, already reusable against a real drive journal.
- **`eval/src/lancet_eval/flatness.py`'s `records_from_run_journal` and `flatness_verdict`**:
  the unmodified analysis path this whole investigation used throughout. Run it directly against
  drive 1's own journal — no replay needed once real data exists.
- **`eval/src/lancet_eval/oi02.py`'s `forensics`, `replay-summary`, and `check-arm`**
  subcommands: `forensics` in particular can be pointed at drive 1's own journal and raw events
  immediately after the drive, the same way it was pointed at the original 06.3.4 journal in
  plan 07's Task 1 — no replay required, it works directly on a real journal.
- **To recreate the replay environment** (if a free replay is ever needed again, e.g. to test
  one of the "not yet tested" factors above without spending money): `scripts/oi02-replay.ps1`
  and its detached launcher `scripts/oi02-replay-launch.ps1` are committed and reusable as-is.
  They need: Docker up (`db` service plus the observability profile), nothing listening on ports
  50051/8080, and — for anything needing the drive-era commit specifically — a git worktree at
  `33e774bd` with the engine and gateway pre-built and `eval/.venv` synced (this plan's own
  worktree was removed as part of cleanup; recreate with
  `git worktree add ../lancet-oi02-drive-era 33e774bd`, then build the engine/gateway binaries
  and `uv sync --project ../lancet-oi02-drive-era/eval` before pointing the replay script at it
  via `-EngineLaunch worktree:<path> -HarnessFrom worktree:<path> -GatewayFrom worktree:<path>`).
  The pre-reconcile store copy and its scratch database were also removed in cleanup; recreate
  via the script's own `-StoreSource pre-reconcile` path, which copies from
  `data/lancedb-eval.pre-06.3.4.1-reconcile/` and restores the scratch DB from
  `data/backups/lancet_eval.pre-06.3.4.1-reconcile.sql` — both rollback layers are kept, untouched.

**Bottom line for whoever plans drive 1:** OI-02 could not be reproduced under controlled
conditions with any combination of factors this plan could test for free or for $0.25. The
mechanism is very likely tied to the actual state of the machine and the engine process during a
real, long-running, hours-long drive — something no short replay can recreate. Drive 1 itself,
properly instrumented and with its telemetry preserved immediately afterward, is now the most
promising remaining source of evidence.
