### Revision rationale for the two edited edge rows (replan pass, REVIEWS.md HIGH-1 and HIGH-2)

Neither row is edited silently, and neither is a re-run of the probe — the probe ran once on the original pass and its five items all resolved `explicit`. Two of the five encoded assumptions that the review invalidated, so their *acceptance criteria* are restated while the edge they cover is unchanged.

- **`empty` was written against the old semantics.** Its original text read "an answerable question whose response returns zero **citations** contributes zero hits and stays in the recall and precision denominators". Under the pre-06.3-08 wire that was the only available reading, and it is exactly the defect the review identified: a query whose retrieval was perfect and whose model simply answered without emitting markers — the shape `allow_model_only` produces by design — would have been recorded as recall 0. With `retrieved_chunks` on the wire, "empty" splits into three genuinely different observations (retrieval ran and found nothing; no `final_response` reached the wire at all; retrieval succeeded but the model cited nothing), and only the first is a measured zero. The restated criterion names all three and binds the discriminator to a wire fact — `snapshot` present versus absent — that `engine/src/workflow/nodes/retrieve.rs` guarantees by assigning `ctx.snapshot` before its zero-evidence notice branch.

**A precision note on the absent case, added on the second replan pass (REVIEWS.md MEDIUM).** An absent `snapshot` is not evidence that retrieval never ran. It means no `final_response` reached the wire, and that has two causes: retrieval was skipped, or a later node — generation is the common one — failed *after* retrieval had already completed, in which case `ctx.snapshot` was set in the engine but the runner emitted `workflow_completed{success: false}` with nothing to carry it. With free-tier generation failures an expected rather than exceptional event, the second cause will be the frequent one. The **disposition is unchanged and correct either way** — `skipped`, never a zero — so no scoring behaviour changes anywhere in this plan set; what changes is only the reason string a reader is given, which must not claim a fact the wire does not support. Plan 06.3-07 already reports the excluded count in its caveats so the shrunken retrieval `n` is visible.
- **`ordering` was written before the k-rule was pinned.** Its original text made wire order and the `rank` field co-authoritative, which is what let plan 06.3-01's tracer (`[:k]` slicing) and plan 06.3-03's metric module (`rank <= k`) diverge — review HIGH-2. The restated criterion pins `rank <= k` as the sole rule and demotes wire order to a tie-break for equal `rank`, matching the single `must_haves` entry now shared across 06.3-01 and 06.3-03. Nothing about the underlying edge changed; the ambiguity did.

The other three rows (`adjacency`, OBS-04 `boundary`, OBS-04 `precision`) are re-examined and unchanged: `adjacency` is about chunk stitching and is orthogonal to which list is scored, and both OBS-04 rows concern the placeholder's status enum and report precision, neither of which the wire change touches.

**Prohibitions.** No `-SPEC.md` also means no `## Prohibitions` section, so the two-stage recall/precision probe was run over OBS-02 and OBS-04. Nine bespoke survivors are authored as descriptor-less `must_haves.prohibitions` items on the plans where they bite: no dimension reports a score it did not compute; the deterministic scoring path acquires no LLM, network or API-key dependency; no cross-corpus aggregate or combined headline figure; the null slice never enters a retrieval or answer denominator; contract, transport and rate-limit failures are never recorded as `0.0`; the judge is never from the generator's family and a judged number never publishes without its `n`, error count, model and prompt version; **no Lancet metric is labelled as, or named after, a quantity it does not measure — restated below**; citations are never re-ordered and `answer_chunk` concatenation is never scored in place of `final_answer.answer`; and no corpus document is committed beyond the sampled questions' referenced articles plus documented distractors, nor without ODC-BY attribution. These are **flagged unverified** in the probe's sense — they carry no `check_*` descriptor — but each has at least one acceptance criterion in its owning plan.

**Restated prohibition — metric naming (replan pass).** The original wording was "no Lancet metric is labelled as the MultiHop-RAG paper's metric". That was satisfied on paper by the plan set (no plan ever writes the paper's metric names) while being violated in substance under the old semantics: a citation-conditioned number published as `evidence_recall_at_4` is precisely the mislabeling the prohibition exists to prevent, and the paper's literal name never had to appear for the reader to be misled. The prohibition is therefore widened from a name-collision rule to a name-meaning rule, in two clauses now carried on the plans where they bite:

1. *(06.3-03, 06.3-05, 06.3-07)* **MUST NOT publish a metric under a name that describes a quantity it does not compute** — in particular, no retrieval-named metric may be computed from `structured_citations`, and any citation-conditioned figure that is published must carry `cited_` in its own name.
2. *(06.3-03, 06.3-07, unchanged in force)* **MUST NOT label a Lancet metric as the MultiHop-RAG paper's metric**, or present Lancet EM/F1 as comparable to the paper's reported accuracy.

Clause 1 is the substantive addition; clause 2 is the original, kept verbatim because it still bites on the `reference_convention_map_at_10` figure. Both are enforced by acceptance criteria in 06.3-03 rather than by review discipline.

Minting them here would duplicate that tooling and drown the bespoke signal.
